Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long do composite patio doors last vs. wood or vinyl?

A: Wood patio doors in high-moisture environments (coastal, humid climates, heavy rainfall areas) typically require replacement within 5-10 years due to bottom frame rot. Vinyl doors last 10-15 years but often develop warping issues in extreme temperatures. MP Doors composite frames maintain structural integrity for 20+ years in harsh conditions. The composite material is non-porous (ASTM D143 testing: less than 0.1% moisture absorption) and dimensionally stable across temperature extremes. Backed by lifetime warranty on frame materials.

Q: Why do patio doors stick, and how does composite prevent this?

A: Three main causes: (1) Frame warping from moisture/heat (50% of cases)—wood absorbs water and swells, vinyl flexes with temperature changes. Composite frames don't absorb moisture and have minimal thermal expansion, maintaining alignment. (2) Debris in track (30%)—affects all doors, solved by cleaning. (3) Roller wear (15%)—MP Doors use stainless steel rollers rated for 50,000+ cycles vs. plastic rollers that fail in 5,000-10,000 cycles.

Q: How much does a patio door affect energy costs?

A: Patio doors account for 10-25% of home heating/cooling loss due to large glass area (40+ sq ft for 72" door). Upgrading from standard glass to Low-E with argon reduces this loss by approximately 40%. In moderate climates: $150-300 annual savings. In extreme climates (Phoenix, Minneapolis): $300-500 annual savings. Over 20 years, energy savings alone often exceed the door's initial cost.

Q: Can I install myself or do I need professional installation?

Improper installation voids warranty and causes 70% of premature failures. MP Doors' pre-hung system makes DIY more feasible than component brands, but professional installation ($400-600) is recommended insurance on a $1,300-2,000 door.
