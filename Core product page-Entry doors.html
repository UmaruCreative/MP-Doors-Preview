Common Problems Solved

"My door is hard to close in summer, drafty in winter"

Wood expands with humidity (up to 8% width gain), shrinks when dry. Composite has less than 0.2% expansion—maintains fit year-round without seasonal adjustment.

"My door looks worn, needs repainting every few years"

Wood degrades from UV and moisture, requires refinishing every 2-3 years ($200-400 each time). Fiberglass is UV-stable and non-porous. Factory finishes last 10+ years. Maintenance: occasional soap and water cleaning.

"I worry about security—frame feels weak when locked"

Wood frames soften from moisture and split at stress points. Composite frames maintain structural rigidity, won't split when deadbolts engage, resist forced entry when properly anchored.

"My steel door is dented and coating is chipping"

Steel dents permanently, conducts heat (causes condensation), coating chips expose rust. Fiberglass won't dent, thermally non-conductive (no condensation), can be refinished if damaged without exposing degradable substrate.

"I want beautiful wood look without constant maintenance"

Wood appearance (deep grain embossing, rich stain colors) without wood's problems. No rot, no splitting, no refinishing. Factory finishes warranted against fading/peeling for years. Visual authenticity from 10 feet—indistinguishable from quality wood.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How does fiberglass compare to wood in appearance?

A: Compression molding captures real wood grain patterns, raised texture, and dimensional shadow lines—not printed or stamped. From normal viewing distance (10+ feet), visually indistinguishable from quality wood. Accepts stain like wood, creating color variation and grain highlighting. Close inspection reveals pattern consistency (fiberglass uses molds, wood has unique grain). Authenticity: 90% of wood's aesthetic with 500% better performance. Wood still best if you accept maintenance requirements; fiberglass if you want wood look without drawbacks.

Q: Will MP Doors really last 20+ years?

A: Conservative estimate based on material science: fiberglass used in boats lasts 30-40 years in saltwater (more aggressive than atmospheric exposure). Composite frames are non-porous—don't rot, minimal UV degradation. Real-world: MP Doors from 1990s still in service (25+ years). Warranty data: less than 2% failure rate over 15 years in proper installations. Failures typically hardware-related (handle replacement), not structural. Compare: wood 5-10 years in harsh climates, steel 15-25 years if rust prevented. Lifetime warranty on composite frame backs this up.

Q: How much energy can I save replacing my old door?

A: Example scenarios:

  • Cold climate (Minneapolis): Old wood door loses $150-250/year in heat. MP Doors reduces to $30-50. Annual savings: $100-200. 20-year savings: $2,000-4,000.
  • Hot climate (Phoenix): Old steel door loses $120-180/year cooling. MP Doors reduces to $40-60. Annual savings: $80-120. 20-year savings: $1,600-2,400.
  • Moderate climate (Atlanta): Annual savings: $50-75. 20-year savings: $1,000-1,500.

Payback period: 7-15 years depending on climate. Plus comfort benefits: fewer drafts, consistent temps, reduced HVAC strain.

Q: Inswing vs. outswing—which should I choose?

A: Determined by codes, architecture, and existing configuration—not just preference. Inswing (most common): Opens INTO home, hinges interior. Required by most residential codes for safety. Better weather protection (rain hits closed face, not hinge gaps). Hinges inaccessible to intruders. Use inswing unless: Outswing: Opens OUT. Better for hurricane zones (wind pushes door into frame), interior space constraints, some ADA applications. Requires special non-removable hinge pins. Check codes before choosing. Cannot easily convert—rough opening is cut for specific handing.

Q: Can I paint or stain my door later?

A: Yes. Repainting smooth doors: Clean, light sand (220-grit), optional bonding primer, exterior acrylic latex paint, 2-3 coats. Lasts 5-7 years. Restaining woodgrain: More complex—strip existing stain, clean, sand, apply gel stain, seal with polyurethane. Professional restaining recommended ($300-600). Color limitations: smooth white → any color (easy); dark stain → light stain (requires complete stripping). Repainting/restaining doesn't void structural warranty but may void finish warranty. More flexibility than steel (must prevent rust) or vinyl (cannot be painted).

Q: How do I know what size I need?

A: Standard residential: 36" x 80" door fits 38" x 82" rough opening (most common). Measure if replacing: measure current door panel (not frame) at top/middle/bottom—use smallest. Standard heights: 80 inches (6'8"). If rough opening visible (remove trim): measure stud to stud, bottom plate to header—use smallest dimensions. Non-standard openings require custom doors or opening modification. Older homes (pre-1960s) often have 30" or 32" doors—MP Doors offers these sizes. When in doubt: professional measurement (often free with installation purchase) prevents expensive ordering mistakes.

Q: What warranty coverage comes with MP Doors?

A: Lifetime warranty (as long as you own home): Fiberglass panel delamination/structural failure, composite frame rot/warping/degradation, material defects. If frame rots (it won't), MP Doors provides replacement regardless of years owned. Limited period: Glass seal (10 years), factory finish (5 years), hardware (1 year). Not covered: Improper installation, normal wear (weatherstripping compression), acts of nature (hurricane damage), cosmetic scratches, glass breakage from impact. Claim process: Register within 30 days, document issue with photos, contact MP Doors, assessment determines coverage. Professional installation strongly recommended for warranty coverage.
